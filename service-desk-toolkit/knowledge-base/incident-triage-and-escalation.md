# Incident triage and escalation guide

## Incident or service request?
- **Incident:** something that was working is broken or degraded (e.g. VPN will not connect)
- **Service request:** a standard ask (e.g. new account, software install, access to a folder)
- **Problem:** the underlying cause behind repeated incidents, managed to prevent recurrence
- **Change:** a planned modification that needs approval

## Priority (example matrix)
| Priority | Impact | Example | Target response / resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Many users or a critical service down | VPN gateway down for all remote staff | 15 min / 4 h |
| P2 | Several users or key function impaired | Application unavailable for one team | 30 min / 8 h |
| P3 | Single user, workaround exists | One user cannot print | 4 h / 24 h |
| P4 | Minor or informational | How-to question | 8 h / 72 h |

These targets are examples. Use your organisation's service levels.

## Ticket lifecycle
New, Assigned, In Progress, On Hold (waiting for user or third party), Resolved, Closed.

## Good ticket notes
Who is affected, what they see (exact error), when it started, what changed, steps tried, and the next action with an owner.

## When to escalate
- Outside your permissions or documented procedure
- A security, access or approval concern (escalate **before** proceeding)
- Multiple users affected
- The SLA is at risk

## Communicating with users
Acknowledge quickly, set expectations, update at agreed intervals, and confirm resolution before closing.
